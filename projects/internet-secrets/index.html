How the Internet Keeps Secrets

Short animated, interactive lessons that teach anyone how the internet moves and protects their information, no cyber background needed.

The idea

Most people use the internet every day without knowing what happens to a message after they press send, or what keeps their password safe. This project is a website of short lessons, each built around one animation, that explains those ideas with everyday comparisons: postcards, mixing paint, fingerprints and a bouncer at the door.

It is written at a high school level for students, parents and the general public, and for the AFROTC cadets I teach as the detachment's Cybersecurity Liaison. Learners step through each animation at their own pace and finish with a five-question self-check quiz.

The four lessons

#LessonWhat the learner sees and doesStatus
1Postcards on the internetA message is chopped into packets and passed hand to hand across a map of routers, readable like a postcard by anyone holding it.Planned
2Mixing secret colorsTwo people each add a private paint color to a shared public one, swap the mixtures, and end up with the same secret color (Diffie-Hellman key exchange).Planned
3Crack the passwordA "blender" shows how changing one letter completely changes a password's fingerprint (hash), which is what websites store instead of the password.Planned
4Guard the gatePackets line up at the network's door, and learners set simple firewall rules, scored on attacks blocked versus real visitors turned away. Time permitting, a flood of traffic (DDoS) round.Planned

What a learner should be able to do

  • Describe how a message is split into packets and passed from router to router.
  • Explain how two people can agree on a shared secret while someone watches everything they send.
  • Explain why websites store a password's hash instead of the password, and why a long passphrase takes far longer to guess than a short password.
  • Describe how a firewall decides which traffic to let in, and why a flood of traffic can knock a website offline even when nothing is stolen.

How I'll know it works: 3 to 5 testers with no cyber background take a short quiz before and after the lessons. The goal is for scores to go up, with 80% (4 of 5) or better on each lesson's quiz.

The full scope, sources and schedule are in the proposal. The lessons will be linked here as each one is finished.
